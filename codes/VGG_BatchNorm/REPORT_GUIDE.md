### 2.2 How Does BN Help Optimization?

Reference: Santurkar et al. (2018) — BN reparametrizes the underlying optimization problem to make its landscape significantly more smooth.

**Three measurements**:

#### (a) Loss Landscape (Lipschitzness)

Method: Train with 4 learning rates [1e-3, 2e-3, 1e-4, 5e-4]. At each step, compute max_loss - min_loss across the 4 models. A narrower band → smoother landscape.

**Result** (insert `loss_landscape_comparison.png`):
- Without BN: mean band width = {X}
- With BN: mean band width = {Y}
- BN reduces band width by **{reduction}%** → confirms smoother loss landscape

#### (b) Gradient Predictiveness

Method: At a fixed training point, measure how well a first-order Taylor expansion predicts the loss change when moving along the gradient direction.

**Result** (insert gradient predictiveness figure from `visualize.py`):
- Without BN: predictiveness error = {X}
- With BN: predictiveness error = {Y}
- BN reduces predictiveness error by **{reduction}%** → gradient direction is more informative

#### (c) Maximum Gradient Difference Over Distance

Method: Measure ||∇L(θ₁) - ∇L(θ₂)|| / ||θ₁ - θ₂|| for nearby parameter pairs.

**Result**: BN reduces the maximum gradient difference, indicating better β-smoothness.

### 2.3 Why BN Works — Summary

Three converging lines of evidence:
1. **Smoother loss** — narrower band across different learning rates
2. **Better gradient predictiveness** — first-order approximation is more accurate
3. **Smaller gradient variation** — gradients change less over small distances

Together, these make gradient descent more effective: each step is more likely to actually reduce the loss, enabling faster and more stable training.
